## Summary

- Each character has a **queue of the last 9 moves that connected**. The more times a move appears in the
  queue, and the more recently, the less damage it deals.
- A move that isn't in the queue at all is **fresh** and gets a **1.05× freshness bonus**.
- A fully staled move (all 9 slots) deals **0.4695×** its base damage.
- **Hitting a shield stales the move** (new in Ultimate). Shield hits count a bit less than real hits, at 0.85×
  the normal reduction for that slot. Perfect shields count too.
- Whiffed moves don't stale. Getting **KO'd resets** all your staleness.
- Staleness has a much **smaller effect on knockback** than on damage, so KO moves stay viable even when stale.
- **Pummels and throws each take a queue slot** (the grab itself likely doesn't), so pummeling before a throw
  is the easiest way to refresh stale moves. 3 new entries take a fully staled move from `0.47×` to `0.72×`
  (see [Refreshing stale moves](#stale-refresh)).

## How the queue works

At the start of a match every move is fresh. When a move **connects** with an opponent, with a shield, or with
anything else that can take damage, it's added to the front of that character's 9-slot queue. Each new move
pushes older entries back until they fall off the end, so using other moves "refreshes" a stale one.

- **Only connecting moves enter the queue.** A move that misses, or hits nothing damageable, doesn't stale.
- **Staleness applies to the whole move**, not to individual hitboxes. Landing a sourspot stales the sweetspot
  too.
- **All versions of the same special move count as one move.** For example, every charge level and both the
  ground and air versions of a chargeable special share one staleness value.
- **Counters and absorbers ignore the staleness of the attack they receive.**
- **KO resets the queue** for the player who was KO'd.

## Damage calculation

A move's reduction is the **sum of the reductors for every queue slot it occupies**:

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1
:label: stale-reductors

* - Queue position
  - Reductor
* - `1`
  - `0.09`
* - `2`
  - `0.08545`
* - `3`
  - `0.07635`
* - `4`
  - `0.0679`
* - `5`
  - `0.05945`
* - `6`
  - `0.05035`
* - `7`
  - `0.04255`
* - `8`
  - `0.03345`
* - `9`
  - `0.025`
* - **All 9 slots**
  - `0.5305`
```

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1
:label: stale-multipliers

* - State
  - Damage multiplier
* - **Fresh** (not in the queue)
  - `1.05×`
* - **Stale**
  - `1 − sum of reductors for occupied slots`
* - **Fully stale** (all 9 slots)
  - `0.4695×`
* - **Slot filled by a shield hit**
  - That slot's reductor × `0.85`
```

**Worked example:** a move sitting in positions 1, 2 and 6 gets 0.09 + 0.08545 + 0.05035 = 0.2258 reduction,
so its next hit deals **77.42%** of base damage.

The freshness bonus is usually too small to matter. The exception the wiki notes is a fresh move being hit back
by a fresh counter.

## Effect on knockback

Ultimate keeps Smash 4's knockback handling. When staleness feeds into the knockback formula, only **30%** of
the staleness modifier (the reduction or the freshness bonus) is applied. So stale KO moves lose much less
knockback than damage, and rage usually covers the rest. You generally don't need to "save" a KO move the way
Brawl players had to.

*Derived:* at full staleness, the damage value used for knockback is about 1 − 0.3 × 0.5305 ≈ **0.84×**, while
the damage actually dealt is 0.4695×.

## What enters the queue

Anything that enters the queue both gets staled itself and pushes every other move one slot back, which
refreshes them. Anything that doesn't enter the queue always deals the same damage and can't refresh anything.

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1

* - Action
  - Enters queue?
  - Notes
* - **Grab** (the catch itself)
  - No *(derived)*
  - Deals no damage, and only moves that deal damage are queued. Not stated explicitly by a source.
* - **Pummel**
  - **Yes**, one entry per pummel
  - The main tool for refreshing stale moves. Each pummel adds one slot.
* - **Throws**
  - **Yes**, one entry per throw
  - Each direction is a separate move, so alternating throws avoids staling one throw.
* - **Grab release**
  - No
  - Never stales
* - **Jab** (hit 1 / 2 / 3)
  - **Yes**
  - Used as a refresh tool. Whether each jab hit counts as a *separate* entry is unverified.
* - **Rapid jab** (flurry attack) and its finisher
  - **Yes**
  - Unverified how the looping hits count (one entry, or one per hit). Lab it before relying on it.
* - **Hitting a shield** (including a perfect shield)
  - **Yes**, at `0.85×` that slot's reductor
  - Ultimate only. Pressuring shields stales your moves.
* - **Hitting a damageable object**
  - **Yes**
  - Eg Wii Fit Trainer's Header hitting her own soccer ball refreshes other moves without touching the opponent.
* - **Whiffs**
  - No
  - A move has to connect.
* - **Taunts**
  - No
  - Except Kazuya's side taunt (Demon's Wrath), which does stale.
* - **Recoil damage**
  - No
  - —
* - **Items** (thrown, shot, or character-generated like Link's Remote Bomb)
  - No
  - Except **swinging** a battering item (Beam Sword, Home-Run Bat, etc.), which stales as its own action.
```

```{note}
Dodge and roll staling (repeated dodges getting slower) is a **separate system**. Dodges never enter this
damage queue, and attacks don't refresh them.
```

**Ways to add entries quickly:**

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1

* - Method
  - Entries
  - Trade-off
* - Pummel before throwing
  - `1` per pummel
  - Every extra pummel gives the opponent more time to mash out.
* - Vary your throws
  - `1` per throw
  - Avoids staling one throw. Doesn't refresh as fast as pummels.
* - Jab (on hit or on shield)
  - `1`+ per jab string
  - Jab on shield counts, at `0.85×`, which only affects how stale jab itself gets.
* - Pummel → throw → jab
  - About `3`–`5`
  - A recommended routine from King K. Rool's wiki page to "almost completely refresh" the queue between
    stocks.
```

A KO resets your whole queue, so refreshing matters most **late in a stock**, when your KO moves are stale.

## Modes and training mode

- Staling (and the freshness bonus) only applies in **multiplayer matches and Home-Run Contest**. In other
  single-player modes and the online practice stage, attacks deal base damage.
- **Training Mode has a Stale Moves toggle** (next to the rage toggle). With the toggle off, moves still enter
  the queue but deal base damage.

## Character-specific notes

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1

* - Character
  - Queue behavior
* - **Pokémon Trainer**
  - Squirtle, Ivysaur and Charizard share one queue. Switching doesn't reset it.
* - **Pyra / Mythra**
  - Share one queue.
* - **Ice Climbers**
  - Share one queue that both climbers affect (details below).
* - **Little Mac**
  - Affected by staling more than most characters, since his weak aerials leave little more than half his
    moveset in regular use. His pummel refreshes quickly: `1.0%`, hitbox frame `1`, interruptible frame `6`.
    His jab is 3 hits plus a rapid jab.
```

**Ice Climbers detail:** Popo updates the queue when his attack's freeze
  frames end, and Nana reads it when her attack hits. If Nana connects during Popo's freeze frames she uses the
  same staleness; if she connects after, her move is one level more stale. If Popo hits before Nana acts, Nana
  doesn't read the queue at all, so her first jab deals base damage unless she hits alone.

## Practical takeaways

*These are interpretations of the mechanics above, not claims from the source.*

- Pressuring shields now costs you: every shielded hit stales that move, just slightly less than a real hit.
- Mixing up your moves in neutral keeps KO options fresh and gets the 1.05× bonus. But since staleness barely
  affects knockback, keeping moves fresh matters more for damage than for KO percent.
- **Little Mac:** late in a stock, pummel 2–3 times on every grab before throwing. Mac's pummel is fast, and
  each one pushes staled KO moves (eg smash attacks) back a slot.
- **Labbing:** turn Stale Moves **off** in Training Mode when measuring damage or KO percents, so results match
  frame data and aren't skewed by what you hit earlier. Turn it on when testing real combo or KO scenarios.
